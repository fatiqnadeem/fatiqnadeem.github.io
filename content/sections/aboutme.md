Welcome! I am an environmental economist at UC Santa Barbara's Bren School of Environmental Science and Management. _**I am on the 2026-2027 Job Market**._

I study why environmental policies fail in the Global South. Using applied microeconomics, I examine how non-state institutions shape environmental outcomes, how weak enforcement changes behavior, and how environmental harms affect public institutions. My research develops better ways to measure environmental damage, strengthen enforcement, and help governments translate policy into results.

I combine environmental economics, causal inference, and remote sensing, and I carry out most of my research in partnership with local institutions, including as an invited member of the Punjab Climate Observatory. My work has been published in *The Economic Journal*, *Science*, and *Energy Policy*, and my research has drawn in competitive funding from the World Bank, the International Growth Centre, the Schmidt Family Foundation and J-PAL.

Previously, I was a Robert S. McNamara Research Fellow at the Development Research Group at the World Bank and a Pre-Doc at Evidence in Governance and Politics (EGAP), UCLA. I hold a BSc. (Honors) in Economics from Lahore University of Management Sciences, Pakistan.
